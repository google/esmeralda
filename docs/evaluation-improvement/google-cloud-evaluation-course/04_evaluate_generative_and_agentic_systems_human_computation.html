The centralized gateway architecture

Deterministic metrics are often insufficient for evaluating generative or agentic systems. Because of this, developers perform informal human evaluations, commonly known as "vibe testing." You enter queries, read the model outputs, and judge if they seem correct.

To build reliable systems, you must evaluate your outputs systematically. You should move from vibe evaluation toward human computation. Systematic human computation differs from vibe testing in both scale and formal practices. These include establishing clear guidelines, enforcing structured rating formats, and maintaining statistical discipline.

Beyond the general importance of human computation to generative and agentic system evaluation, there is no substitute for systematic human computation when tasks involve highly subjective criteria, when a system has compliance or legal risks, or when scaling evaluation to autoraters.

Note that human computation is distinct from Human-in-the-Loop (HITL). HITL is a system design pattern where humans intervene in active runs of a deployed system. Human computation, conversely, is an evaluation and data collection practice.

To address human annotator subjectivity, you must measure agreement.

This is a complex discipline itself. Seemingly simple metrics, like "percent agreement," are misleading because they do not account for random agreement by chance.

If you find yourself managing large human computation efforts, it's worth investigating agreement metrics like Cohen's Kappa (popular) and Krippendorff's Alpha (gold standard).

Ultimately, human computation serves a dual role, namely, it is: 

  • a critical validation gateway for production, and

  • the primary tool for calibrating autoraters both initially and on an ongoing basis.
