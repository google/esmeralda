Generative and agentic systems naturally accumulate prompt engineering technical debt. Early in development, writing quick prompt instructions is a cheap way to get results. However, over time, these prompt adjustments accumulate debt (usually, incrementally) that you must unwind during an upgrade.

Unwinding prompt engineering debt is especially important when migrating from older model generations. Older models required highly customized, complex prompts to achieve basic quality. Newer models possess significantly better reasoning capabilities, and legacy prompts can confuse newer models and in some cases degrade performance.

As you work with legacy generative and agentic systems, you must also watch for factuality drift. This occurs when outputs that were correct in the past are now incorrect because underlying facts changed.

For example, if your HR chatbot references an outdated policy document, its correct output is flagged as a regression unless you update your evaluation datasets.

Factuality drift is distinct from data drift, which refers to shifts in the distribution of user queries entering the system.

The centralized gateway architecture

Upgrading a generative and agentic system without operational tooling is labor-intensive, slow, and prone to error. Because foundation models evolve rapidly, you face deprecation timelines repeatedly. Treating each upgrade as a manual project is unsustainable.

GenAIOps (LLMOps) is the collection of tools and practices that make building and running generative and agentic systems faster, easier, and less prone to failure. The benefits of GenAIOps extend to model upgrades, making them repeatable and fast instead of a one-off task, allowing you to move quickly to new models and stay cutting-edge, getting a recurring competitive advantage.
