Building a generative or agentic system without an evaluation plan leads to disjointed testing, misaligned expectations, and uncalibrated metrics. A structured evaluation plan acts as a blueprint, mapping organizational KPIs to specific system goals and measurable metrics.

In this lesson, you'll explore how to create an evaluation plan that connects organizational priorities to technical metrics, how to communicate evaluation progress effectively, and how human agency impacts evaluation.

The centralized gateway architecture

To build enterprise-grade generative and agentic systems, you cannot just figure out evaluation as you go. You need an evaluation plan to ensure you do not fall victim to the core challenges of generative development: non-determinism, capability variability, and the oracle problem.

The centralized gateway architecture

An evaluation plan is not necessarily a plan of the evaluation you will implement. Instead, the plan provides a map. It allows you to prioritize engineering resources, making informed decisions that anchor technical work back to organizational goals. This makes the plan highly useful for project scheduling, budgeting, and setting realistic stakeholder expectations.

The centralized gateway architecture

As you hill climb, technical metrics improve. However, decision makers rarely care about raw numbers. They want to understand how these metrics show progress towards organizational goals.

To communicate progress, map your metric improvements to potential KPI impacts. Instead of reporting a five percent accuracy increase, explain how that change shows progress towards the organizational goal. This requires creative aggregation of your evaluation data to tell a compelling story.

The centralized gateway architecture

The way humans interact with your system determines how you must design your evaluation plan.

There are three primary agency models:

Click each flashcard to learn more.

These agency models often overlap. For example, a customer support chatbot where the customer can escalate to a human operator is in a gray area between HOTL and HITL.

Your chosen agency model dictates your evaluation priorities:

  • Evaluating Automated Systems: Focus on strong guardrails, extensive regression testing, and comprehensive safety and security evaluations. Because the system operates without human supervision, require strict $pass^k$ thresholds.

  • Evaluating HITL Systems: Focus on human-AI collaboration metrics. Measure time-to-decision and user correction rates. For HITL systems, the efficiency of the human-AI pairing and the particulars of the UX can be more critical than raw output quality.

  • Evaluating HOTL Systems: Focus on escalation precision and recall, trigger calibration, and random sampling protocols to audit non-escalated outputs.
